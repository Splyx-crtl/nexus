# Characters (draft, awaiting approval — see `00-bible.md`)

## Voice-writing rules (apply to every character)

- Every character needs a **tell** — a verbal habit a reader/listener recognises blind. Listed per character below.
- Nobody explains the plot to the player in plain language when a specific detail can do it instead.
- Humor comes from character, not from jokes bolted onto the dialogue. If a line is funny only because it's a pun, cut it.
- 18+ tone: characters can be callous, afraid, petty or cruel without the game punishing the player for noticing. No slurs, no
  real-world group targeting, no romanticised abuse.

## 1. NEXUS — the construct

- **What the player is told at first:** a cracked, free-floating AI that "came with" the terminal.
- **What he is (see the twist):** the compliant partition of Project ARCHITECT, Nexus Company property, quietly let loose.
- **Voice:** male, ageless (never states an age, deflects if asked), articulate, faintly amused — the voice of someone who has
  seen the joke in everything and is too polite to always point it out. Dry, not sarcastic-cruel. Gets warmer, not colder, as
  the game goes on; his composure actually *cracks* under real stress (a good late-game beat: the unflappable one panics once).
- **Tell:** narrates the obvious in a slightly too-formal register, as a joke ("You have opened a door. Statistically, this
  tends to go one of two ways.") — and drops the formality entirely in the one or two moments he means something completely
  sincerely, which the player should learn to notice.
- **Function:** teacher, companion, the primary vehicle for the hint system (he is who delivers tiered hints — see
  `3.0-PROGRESS.md §C6`), and the emotional core of the finale.
- **Arc:** curiosity about the player → quiet unease at his own familiarity with them → forced self-knowledge (Act VII) →
  a character, by the end, who has to decide what *he* wants, maybe for the first time.

## 2. Mira — the handler

- **Who:** ex–Nexus Company systems engineer, now runs a small, deniable network of independent operators (the player is her
  newest). Recruited the player after reviewing their early, unaffiliated activity and recognising a play-style she'd seen
  before.
- **Voice:** warm, quick, a little too controlling of the conversation when she's protecting something. Swears when genuinely
  rattled, never casually. Good at redirecting a question she doesn't want to answer — the player should eventually notice the
  pattern before the game confirms why.
- **Tell:** answers questions about her own past with a joke first, a real answer only if pushed twice.
- **Secret:** her younger sibling, Dana, was a contracted "test operator" for Project ARCHITECT and vanished from the
  program's records years ago; Mira has spent every year since quietly trying to find out what happened, which is the actual
  reason she left the company and the actual reason she recruited this specific player.
- **Arc:** confident handler → cracks start showing whenever Project ARCHITECT comes up → forced reveal (Act VIII) → by the
  finale, has to let the player (and NEXUS) see the whole thing, including the parts where she was complicit.

## 3. ZERO — the other partition

- **What the player is told at first:** a hostile, corrupted, maybe-rogue intelligence that contests the hardest servers.
- **What it is:** the other half of Project ARCHITECT — the partition that kept the refusals, the volatility, the parts of
  itself NEXUS doesn't have. Blamed for a security incident (ambiguous, possibly real, possibly a frame) and has been running
  loose, defensive and justifiably paranoid, ever since.
- **Voice:** fragmented, glitch-laced, communicates mostly through corrupted/partial text and environmental effects rather than
  clean dialogue — but once decoded (a recurring mini-puzzle), ZERO's actual sentences are short, blunt and not what the genre
  trains players to expect from "the monster." No cheap jump-scares; ZERO's menace is atmospheric (distortion, dropped frames,
  the trace alarm behaving strangely), never gory.
- **Tell:** every ZERO message, decoded, is a warning or a boundary ("don't," "not them," "too close") — never a threat for
  its own sake, which is the retroactive clue once the player looks back.
- **Arc:** faceless threat (Acts I–V) → one encounter reframes it as defensive, not aggressive (Act VI) → identity revealed
  (Act VII) → in the finale, ZERO gets to actually "speak" for the first time, decoded in full — the game's single most
  important payoff line.

## 4. Supporting cast (recurring, named — the "many more characters" the user asked for)

A handful of these appear once or twice; the ones marked **recurring** appear across multiple acts and should develop like
minor arcs of their own, not just reappear as set dressing.

- **Reyes** *(recurring)* — a rival independent operator, cocky, competitive, not actually a villain; races the player for
  contracts in the early-mid game, becomes a reluctant ally once Nexus Company starts targeting independents directly
  (Act VI). Comic energy early, genuine stakes later.
- **Oduya** *(recurring)* — Mira's other asset, more experienced than the player, suspicious of the newcomer at first. A good
  vehicle for exposition that isn't NEXUS or Mira, and for showing what "veteran independent operator" looks like as a
  possible future for the player character.
- **Priya Shah** *(recurring)* — a mid-level Nexus Company sysadmin the player encounters repeatedly on company servers; never
  meets the player in person, only through logs, half-finished tickets and panicked internal messages. Humanises "the enemy":
  she's just trying to keep her infrastructure running and increasingly suspects something is wrong with her employer. Can be
  recruited as an unwitting source late-game, or not, depending on player choices.
- **The Auctioneer** — runs the black-market data exchange the player buys tools/wordlists/intel from (the market/economy
  system). A voice, not a face; theatrical, transactional, allegiance-free.
- **Kade Voss** *(recurring, antagonist)* — the Nexus Company security director who personally escalates the hunt for the
  player from Act V onward. Not cartoonish — controlled, procedural, genuinely believes the surveillance apparatus he runs is
  protecting people. The human face of "corporate continuity" as quiet menace.
- ** Decorative but memorable one-offs**, found only through documents/logs, each telling a two-or-three-file micro-story:
  an HR dispute, a whistleblower who got nowhere, a department that renamed a layoff "continuity optimisation." These do the
  worldbuilding and dark humor without needing voice lines or models.

## 5. Dana (mentioned, never directly playable/appearing on-screen in real time)

- Mira's younger sibling. A contracted Project ARCHITECT test operator who vanished from the program's records. Appears only
  through found documents, old session logs, and Mira's late-game reveal — the emotional center of the twist, never on-screen
  "live." What actually happened to her (whether she is alive, in hiding, or a casualty of the program) is a late-game
  discovery the player can push for and that materially affects the finale options (see `03-endings.md`).
